# LexLight — Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
Non-lawyers across a wide range: stressed individuals who just received a contract, freelancers wanting a fast second opinion, and first-time renters or employees encountering legal language for the first time. Used on laptops at home, often under time pressure, with moderate anxiety about what they're agreeing to. They don't have a lawyer. They have this tool.

### Brand Personality
**Protective. Clear. Steady.**

Emotionally, the user should finish an analysis feeling *safe and protected* — as though the tool caught something important that they would have missed. The interface should radiate competence without intimidation. It is not a legal firm. It is not a startup. It is a trusted tool that happens to understand legal documents.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Calm utility tool.** Feels like a thoughtfully designed professional instrument — clear hierarchy, warm neutrals, no decorative excess. Functional beauty. Think: a well-made reference book, a good quality magnifying glass, an archivist's desk. Not minimal for minimalism's sake, but stripped down to what earns its place.

- **Theme**: Light mode. Users are reading contracts during the day, often stressed. Light mode reads as clear, honest, and unhurried.
- **Existing brand colors to honor**: Dark near-black (#0e0f16) for hero/structural elements, warm orange (#c8791a) as the single accent, warm off-white (#faf9f6, #f5f0e8) for surfaces.
- **Existing fonts to honor**: Bricolage Grotesque (display) + Barlow (body) — already a strong, distinctive pairing. These are keepers.

**Anti-references (must NOT look like):**
- Generic SaaS: no purple-blue gradients, no feature icon grids, no hero illustrations
- AI product aesthetic: no glowing UI mockups, no "Powered by AI" badges, no neon-on-dark
- Consumer fintech: no pastel palettes, no rounded-everything, no friendly illustration style
- Law firm: no dark navy + gold, no gavels, no formal-old-world serifs

### Design Principles
1. **Competence over decoration** — Every visual element must serve comprehension or trust, never novelty. Remove anything that doesn't earn its place.
2. **Calm in the face of complexity** — The interface absorbs the anxiety that contracts create. White space, clear hierarchy, and measured pacing communicate: "we've got this."
3. **The tool disappears into the task** — Interactions feel inevitable, not designed. The user's attention should be on the contract, not the UI chrome.
4. **Warmth without whimsy** — The warm neutrals and orange accent humanize the tool without making it feel casual or playful. Legal content is serious; the tone is helpful-serious.
5. **Privacy as personality** — The "never stored, no account" positioning is a design asset. It should show up in microcopy and information hierarchy, not just in a tiny footer footnote.
